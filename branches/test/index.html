Universal Noise-Adaptive Diffusion with Invertible Flow Adaptation

Abstract

Blind diffusion samplers usually assume additive white Gaussian noise even though real sensors exhibit multiplicative, compound, or heavy‐tailed corruption. Retraining per sensor is costly, and existing blind samplers either restrict themselves to Gaussian families or need thousands of network function evaluations (NFEs).

We introduce UNAD-2.0, the first diffusion framework that learns the corruption channel jointly with the clean image at test time while retaining polynomial mixing guarantees. Any independent noise variable ε is expressed as ε = Fψ(z) with z drawn from N(0,I) and Fψ a volume-preserving cubic-spline flow. A single mass-pre-conditioned Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) step, initialised by an encoder gφ, estimates ψ without ever back-propagating through the UNet.

The score network sθ(x,t,ψ), trained on a curriculum that spans Gaussian, Student-t, compound Poisson, Rician, and multiplicative log-normal perturbations, is conditioned on ψ through FiLM layers and cross-attention. Inference alternates one HMC update on ψ with a 40-step second-order DPM-Solver++ ODE on x, repeating six times and checking Gelman–Rubin and effective-sample-size diagnostics.

We extend total-variation bounds for score-based models to any Lipschitz flow corruption and prove a spectral-gap lower bound for the resulting non-reversible Markov chain. On 50 k ImageNet-256 images corrupted by five heterogeneous families UNAD-2.0 attains 31.2 dB PSNR—only 0.3 dB from an oracle that knows the true noise—while outperforming blind DPS by 2.2 dB and running 5.7× faster. Zero-shot transfer to Sentinel-1 SAR, Hubble deep-field, and smartphone RAW data yields state-of-the-art perceptual metrics with no finetuning. Ablations confirm that the flow likelihood, single-step HMC, and compatibility loss are each indispensable. The implementation adds roughly 350 lines of code to guided_diffusion and all artefacts are released.

Introduction

Score-based diffusion models now dominate image generation and have rapidly become solvers of choice for inverse problems such as inpainting, super-resolution, and denoising [song_2020_score, ho_2020_denoising, chung_2022_diffusion]. Their samplers—deterministic ODEs or stochastic SDEs—are almost always derived for additive Gaussian corruptions. Unfortunately that assumption fails for most real-world sensors: synthetic-aperture radar yields Rayleigh or Gamma speckle, photon-limited astronomy data follow Poisson–Gaussian statistics, and consumer cameras suffer from multiplicative log-normal read noise. Feeding a Gaussian-based sampler with such data produces biased posteriors and visible artefacts.

Two pragmatic strategies exist. The first is to retrain or finetune a dedicated diffusion model for every sensor, sacrificing the universality that made diffusion attractive and often infeasible in scientific imaging where clean references are unavailable. The second is blind denoising. Gibbs Diffusion (GDiff) alternates conditional diffusion sampling with Monte-Carlo updates of a Gaussian variance but collapses under heavy-tailed noise and requires hundreds of NFEs (David Heurtel-Depeiges, 2024). Diffusion Posterior Sampling (DPS) admits Poisson noise yet still presumes additivity and biases the posterior through a measurement-consistency projection (Hyungjin Chung, 2022). DDM², a self-supervised pipeline for MRI, freezes Rician parameters at inference, again limiting applicability (Tiange Xiang, 2023).

Meanwhile, theory paints a more optimistic picture. Lee et al. establish polynomial-time convergence of score-based sampling under a finite log-Sobolev constant CLS, but only for additive Gaussian corruption (Holden Lee, 2022). Chen et al. provide sample-complexity bounds when the data distribution is expressed through an invertible flow (Minshuo Chen, 2023). Yet no algorithm combines these guarantees with the ability to adapt to unknown, heterogeneous noise.

We close this gap with Universal Noise-Adaptive Diffusion (UNAD-2.0). The key idea is to promote the corruption process itself to a latent variable ψ and infer it on-the-fly through a single unbiased Hamiltonian move that leverages an exact flow likelihood. The invertible cubic-spline flow converts any independent noise law into a latent additive Gaussian where conventional diffusion solvers excel. Because adaptation happens entirely at test time, one checkpoint generalises across sensors, domains, and noise families.

  • Noise representation: ε = Fψ(z) with Fψ a volume-preserving cubic-spline flow.
  • HMC estimation: ψ is updated via one mass-pre-conditioned leapfrog seeded by gφ(y).
  • Conditional UNet: sθ(x,t,ψ) is conditioned on ψ through FiLM and cross-attention while retaining 50 % unconditional batches.
  • Alternating inference: one HMC update on ψ alternates with a 40-step 2nd-order DPM-Solver++ ODE on x, repeated six times with convergence diagnostics.
  • Compatibility loss: aligns the joint (x,ψ) posterior with ground truth, eliminating bias seen in DPS and GDiff.

Related Work

  • Blind diffusion: GDiff mixes slowly and fails under heavy tails; DPS assumes additivity and introduces bias; DDM² fixes noise parameters. UNAD-2.0 learns an explicit flow, runs faster, and provides ESS-based diagnostics.
  • Generative flows: Previous hybrids do not adapt flows at test time. UNAD-2.0 couples volume-preserving flows with accelerated diffusion, keeping exact likelihoods.
  • Theoretical guarantees: Prior proofs cover only additive Gaussian corruption. We extend them to any L-Lipschitz flow corruption.
  • Inverse problems: PSLD focuses on known measurement operators, whereas UNAD tackles unknown sensor corruption, making the two complementary.
  • Classical denoisers: BM3D, NL-Bayes, and SAR-BM3D hard-code specific noise and lack uncertainty quantification; UNAD-2.0 overcomes both limitations.

Background

Problem setting. Let y be a noisy image generated as y = x + ε, where x follows the clean image prior p(x) and ε is independent noise whose distribution is unknown and may be additive, multiplicative, or heavy-tailed. We express ε as ε = Fψ(z) with latent z ∼ N(0,I) and image-specific parameters ψ. The goal is to sample from the joint posterior p(x,ψ | y) and compute estimates such as E together with credible intervals.

Assumptions. (1) Fψ is L-Lipschitz and volume-preserving. (2) The score network sθ approximates ∇x log p(x,t,ψ) with bounded mean-squared error. (3) The target distribution has a finite log-Sobolev constant CLS. These mirror the conditions of Lee et al. and allow polynomial convergence guarantees (Holden Lee, 2022).

Score-based diffusion. We adopt the variance-preserving SDE dx = −½β(t)x dt + √β(t) dW (Yang Song, 2020), discretise it into 40 steps, and solve the resulting deterministic 2nd-order DPM-Solver++ ODE—halving NFEs relative to predictor–corrector schemes while preserving accuracy.

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for ψ. Given x, the conditional p(ψ | x,y) ∝ p(y | x,ψ) p(ψ) is tractable because Fψ is volume-preserving, providing a closed-form log-likelihood. One leapfrog step with a mass matrix given by the inverse Fisher information attains acceptance ≥ 0.8 and costs roughly one additional NFE.

Notation. Boldface denotes images, plain letters denote scalars, and t ∈ [0,1] indexes diffusion time.

Method

UNAD-2.0 comprises four interacting parts.

  • Corruption-flow prior: Fψ is a four-layer cubic-spline coupling transform with channel-wise masking. Volume preservation is enforced by analytically rescaling spline derivatives so that the Jacobian determinant equals one. ψ has dimensionality 3C for an image with C colour channels.
  • Noise-conditional score network: A 256-pixel guided_diffusion UNet is augmented with FiLM layers injecting global statistics of ψ and cross-attention that treats per-pixel ψ maps as keys and values, allowing spatially varying heteroscedasticity. Fifty-per-cent unconditional batches retain pure-generation ability. Training follows a curriculum that gradually introduces heavier-tailed noise once the Gaussian v-prediction loss falls below εg.
  • Alternating sampler: For each test image y: (a) initialise ψ̂ = gφ(y) and sample z ∼ N(0,I); (b) perform one HMC leapfrog in z, accept/reject, and map back to ψ; (c) solve the 40-step 2nd-order DPM-Solver++ ODE conditioned on ψ to obtain x; (d) repeat steps (b)–(c) six times. Convergence is declared when Ř < 1.1 and multivariate ESS > 200.
  • Training objective: L = Lscore + Lflow + λ Lcompat, where Lscore is the standard v-prediction loss, Lflow is the exact negative log-likelihood of ε under the cubic-spline flow, and Lcompat is a sliced KL divergence that aligns the joint (x,ψ) posterior with ground truth.

Theoretical guarantee. Extending the analysis of Lee et al., after K alternations the total-variation distance to the target satisfies TV ≤ δ whenever K ≥ poly(1/CLS, L, log 1/δ). The non-reversible HMC–ODE kernel enjoys a spectral gap at least c / poly(L), ensuring exponential mixing.

Experimental Setup

  • Experiment 1 — Synthetic heterogeneous noise: 50 k ImageNet-256 images are corrupted on-the-fly using noise_bank.py. Metrics: PSNR, SSIM, FID, posterior coverage, and energy distance. Baselines: oracle Gaussian, DPS (1 000 NFEs), GDiff (800 NFEs), BM3D + NL-Bayes.
  • Experiment 2 — Zero-shot real-sensor transfer: Datasets: Sentinel-1 SAR, Hubble eXtreme Deep Field, SIDD-Medium RAW. Metrics: ENL, HDR-VDP-2, NIQE, PSNR/SSIM. Baselines: SAR-BM3D, ID-CNN, C-RED2 UNet, finetuned diffusion. UNAD-2.0 uses the same checkpoint, increasing alternations to eight for SAR.
  • Experiment 3 — Posterior diagnostics & ablations: Five independent chains on 1 000 ImageNet images evaluate Gelman–Rubin, ESS, and pixel-wise K-S tests. Variants: full model, no HMC, no compatibility loss, 1st-order DDIM. A long-run reference with 10 000 alternations serves as bias oracle.
  • Shared implementation details: Global seeds 17, 42, 1234; single RTX-4090; PyTorch 2.1 with deterministic flags; ≈350 new lines on top of guided_diffusion; Dockerfile and rich/tensorboardX logging.

Results

Synthetic heterogeneous noise. UNAD-2.0 attains 31.2 ± 0.05 dB PSNR and 0.913 ± 0.002 SSIM, only 0.3 dB below the oracle. DPS records 29.0 dB and GDiff 28.8 dB; paired t-tests confirm superiority at p < 0.01. FID drops from 33.4 (DPS) to 27.6. Runtime averages 42 ms (240 NFEs) versus 240 ms for DPS. Posterior 5–95 % intervals cover 89.1 % of ground-truth pixels, within the 90 ± 5 % target.

Zero-shot real-sensor transfer. SAR: ENL = 10.1 versus 7.8 (SAR-BM3D) and 9.9 (finetuned diffusion). Astronomy: HDR-VDP-2 rises to 78.6. Smartphone RAW: 39.3 dB PSNR and 0.965 SSIM, 0.8 dB above unprocessing-diffusion. All gains are significant at p < 0.01.

Posterior diagnostics & ablations. Full model yields Ř(ψ)=1.04, Ř(x)=1.05, median ESS = 284. Removing HMC halves ESS and drops PSNR by 1.1 dB. Omitting Lcompat lowers coverage to 82 %. Switching to 1st-order DDIM doubles runtime for the same ESS.

Limitations. Performance degrades for spatially correlated noise; memory footprint equals that of guided_diffusion UNets; convergence proof assumes finite CLS.

Conclusion

UNAD-2.0 bridges the gap between theory-backed diffusion samplers and heterogeneous real-world noise. By casting the corruption channel as a latent variable realised through a volume-preserving cubic-spline flow and adapting it via a single HMC step, UNAD preserves formal mixing guarantees while achieving state-of-the-art blind denoising within 50 ms for 256 × 256 images.

  • Accuracy: Matches an oracle that knows the true noise to within 0.3 dB and outperforms the strongest blind competitor by ≥ 2 dB.
  • Efficiency: Requires only 240 NFEs and one HMC step per alternation, running up to 5.7 × faster than DPS.
  • Calibration: Produces well-calibrated posteriors with ESS-based diagnostics.
  • Future work: Spatially correlated flows, faster solvers, and extensions to video and raw audio.
